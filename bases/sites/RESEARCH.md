# What the research says (October 2026)

Collected while writing the website skills (`~/auto/.claude/skills`: `local-seo`, `local-service-copy`,
`site-kit`, `site-audit`). These are the parts that bear on how Automaton sells and builds. Strength:
**wide** = many practitioners agree; **survey**; **vendor** = a seller's own numbers; **single** = one source.
Reddit blocks the research tools, so practitioner views come from Hacker News, the Local Search Forum
(LSF) and ContractorTalk instead.

## Selling sites to trades

- A cheap one-off site only works with something recurring behind it (hosting, edits, profile and review
  help). Commenters describe $3–5k up front plus $500–1k a month as the model that lasts. *several; the
  thread is from 2019.* $299 AI-built sites are now common, so the $299 Launch competes on speed and
  outcome, and Premium has to be sold on calls and booked jobs, not on the site. *several*
- What owners pay for is the phone ringing, plus the Google profile and reviews they don't want to run
  themselves. *several*
- Some contractors say referrals and a Google Business Profile matter more than any website; others get
  half their leads from it. Lead with the profile and reviews help when an owner is sceptical.
  *ContractorTalk, mixed*
- A fixed price needs a written "not included" line. *single*

## What makes a trade site convert

- A tap-to-call number that stays on screen on phones. *wide (the numbers are vendor)*
- Recent, dated reviews, with the owner replying to each: in BrightLocal's 2026 survey 74% want reviews
  from the last three months, and half are put off by templated replies. *survey*
- The phone and the form on every service page: blog posts can outrank a service page, but the leads
  come through the service page. *single, LSF*
- Real job photos and the licence near the call button; "24/7" only if it's true. *vendor, consistent*
- Ask callers what frustrated them on the site: the cheapest way to find what's losing calls. *single, LSF*

## Bots, booking and speed

- In a secret-shopper study of 450+ home-service companies, 95% didn't answer a web lead within five
  minutes. Answering first is the gap the kit closes. *ProRemodeler; direction agreed, exact figures vary*
- The bot's value is after hours, as a way to get a call back: don't guess, take a name and number, and
  always offer a person. Many customers dislike AI service, so it must never stand in front of the phone.
  *several; Gartner/SurveyMonkey figures second-hand*
- Booking channel: in one survey of 1,000 homeowners, 50% prefer to phone, 24% to text, 12% to book
  online (vendor surveys claim far more for online). Keep the phone first; Cal.com suits estimates, not
  emergencies. *survey, conflicting*

## Local SEO

- For the map pack, the Google Business Profile and reviews outweigh the website, and directory listings
  matter least (Whitespark 2026 survey of 47 experts). *survey*
- City pages work only with real local content; clones with the town swapped are doorway pages. *wide, LSF*
- Schema clarifies but doesn't lift rankings by itself; service-area settings change where a profile shows,
  not how it ranks; keyword-stuffed business names rank but get suspended. *LSF, Search Engine Land tests*
