# Niche shortlist

Five niches for the channel, scored against the brief's criteria: evergreen, real source material, ad-friendly, a gap Markus can fill, and something that doesn't turn into the same video every week. Written 2026-10-25, from what Markus builds and from web research; money figures are rough (below).

## The five, best fit first

### 1. Building with AI: "I built X with AI" logs
- **Why it fits**: Markus really builds these (Automaton's pipeline, JARVIS, Roblox tooling). Screen recordings of his own builds are original footage no one else has, which is exactly what keeps a faceless channel on the right side of YouTube's "inauthentic content" rule.
- **Audience and money**: tech and business viewers; RPM in the higher group (see below).
- **Source material**: his own repos and sessions. Never short of it.
- **Risks**: the tools move fast, so videos date; crowded at the "AI news" end, less so for real builds.
- **First three videos**: how JARVIS builds himself while he's away; a client website from nothing to live, sped up; an AI voice assistant at a desk in 3D.

### 2. Website redesigns: before and after
- **Why it fits**: the Automaton pipeline produces the visuals, so each video is original work and doubles as a showreel for clients.
- **Audience and money**: small-business owners, designers; mid-to-high RPM; the real payoff is leads for Automaton.
- **Risks**: use only sites Markus has permission to show, or invented example businesses; no client data on screen.
- **First three videos**: a plumber's site redone; five mistakes on local-business sites; a personal-brand site in a day.

### 3. Real estate explainers
- **Why it fits**: ties to the real-estate walkthrough stream; one of the higher-paying topics; plenty of public data (prices, rates, rules).
- **Audience and money**: buyers and sellers; high RPM.
- **Risks**: facts must be checked and sourced; local rules vary; never presented as legal or financial advice.
- **First three videos**: what a listing video should show; how to read a listing; what AI can and can't do for a listing.

### 4. Roblox developer tutorials
- **Why it fits**: Markus builds Roblox games and has the studio queue's real findings (what broke in play-tests, and how it was fixed).
- **Audience and money**: aspiring developers, not young children; moderate RPM. Aim it at developers so the videos aren't "made for kids", which turns off personalised ads and comments.
- **Risks**: Roblox changes; keep game footage to Markus's own games.
- **First three videos**: a play-test finding and its fix; making a drivable town; why kids' games get abandoned in the first minute.

### 5. Futures and market education (MNQ)
- **Why it's last**: finance pays the most per view, and it's what Markus trades. But it carries the most risk: advice rules, and YouTube's tighter treatment of AI-made presenters on finance (see below). Monitoring and education only, no signals, no performance claims.
- **If it's done**: a human-led channel with his own screen and voice, not an AI persona.
- **First three videos**: what MNQ is and how a contract works; reading a day's session without hindsight; risk per trade.

## Recommendation
Start with **1, and run 2 beside it**: both are made from work Markus is already doing, so nothing is faked, and 2 feeds Automaton. Keep 5 for later, and human-led.

## What to check before relying on this
- **Money figures**: sources conflict and many are SEO or sales pages. Rough picture only: finance and business highest, technology and education next, entertainment and gaming lowest; viewer country matters a lot (US viewers pay several times more). Check real RPM in YouTube Studio once a channel exists.
- **Policy**: YouTube renamed "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content" in July 2025, framed as a clarification. AI use isn't banned; mass-produced, near-identical, template videos are what's at risk. One article dated July 2026 reports tighter rules from 16 July for channels that lean on AI and templates, and for AI personas discussing health, finance or legal topics. I couldn't confirm that against YouTube's own Help Center: read it there before building anything on it.
- **Partner Program thresholds** (subscribers and watch hours): not confirmed in my searches; check the Help Center.
